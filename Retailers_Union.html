Know Your Rights

The law is on your side. Here are the protections every retail worker should know.

Right to Organize (NLRA)

Under the National Labor Relations Act, you have the right to form or join a union, bargain collectively, and engage in protected concerted activity—without retaliation[reference:7].

Right to Be Paid for All Hours Worked

Bag checks, closing duties, and mandatory meetings are work time. Wage theft—including unpaid overtime and break denial—is illegal. 40% of supermarket workers have experienced it[reference:8].

Right to Predictive Scheduling

If you work in a Fair Workweek jurisdiction (e.g., NYC, Chicago, Seattle, Oregon), your employer must post schedules 14 days in advance and pay a premium for last-minute changes[reference:9].

Protection from Wage Deductions

Employers generally cannot deduct register shortages or breakage from your pay unless they can prove dishonesty or gross negligence[reference:10].

How to Organize Your Workplace

Change starts with a conversation. Here’s the proven path to forming a union.

1

Talk to Co-Workers

Start low-stakes conversations about wages, scheduling, and respect. Find out who shares your concerns[reference:16].

2

Build an Organizing Committee

Gather a small, trusted group to chart issues, plan actions, and support each other[reference:17].

3

Sign Union Cards

Once 30% of workers sign authorization cards, you can file for a union election with the NLRB[reference:18].

4

Win the Election

Vote as a workplace. A majority wins the right to bargain a contract.

5

Negotiate Your Contract

Your bargaining committee negotiates wages, benefits, and protections. You vote to ratify[reference:19].

6

Enforce Your Rights

Your union stays on the job to enforce the contract, handle grievances, and hold management accountable.
